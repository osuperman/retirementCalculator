## 2. 🔴 Logic Bugs (bounded, but real)

**C1. Funded plans can be reported as "depleted" to the AI assistant.**
- Where: `summary.depleted` set from raw per-year `unmetCashFlow > 1` ([App.jsx:2881](src/App.jsx:2881), [:3912](src/App.jsx:3912)); shipped verbatim to the chat backend in `buildChatProfile` ([App.jsx:6444](src/App.jsx:6444)).
- What happens: the gross-up solver converges to <$1/year, and IRMAA float spending can leave ~$1 of "unmet" cash flow in a year; the parity run (`REFERENCE_DISCREPANCIES.md` B-18) shows `depleted: true` on a plan ending with **$3.07M**. Every UI consumer correctly gates on the materiality threshold (`hasMaterialUnmetCashFlow`, [App.jsx:6898](src/App.jsx:6898)), so the banner is right — but the Ask AI tab receives the raw flag and can tell the user their funded plan fails.
- Fix: set `depleted` using the same materiality threshold (or a ≥$1,000 cumulative gate), or strip/replace the field in the chat profile.

**C2. ACA at exactly 400% FPL charges 2.1% instead of 9.96%.**
- Where: [App.jsx:433–443](src/App.jsx:433). The cliff test is `fplRatio > 4.0`, but the band lookup uses `fplRatio < high`, so ratio == 4.0 matches no band and falls back to the first band's 2.1%.
- Probe: `pct(3.99) = 0.0996`, `pct(4.00) = 0.021`, `pct(4.01) = full cost`. Statutorily, income ≤ 400% FPL is eligible at 9.96% (§36B). Latent (requires float-exact 4.0) but a wrong branch; one-line fix: make the final band's upper bound inclusive.

No material errors were found in the core tax, RMD, Social Security, or growth math.

## 3. ⚠️ Needs Update

**U1. FPL base year is stale.** [App.jsx:408–413](src/App.jsx:408) projects the **2024** guidelines ($15,060 + $5,380) by user inflation. 2026 ACA eligibility actually uses the **2025** guidelines: **$15,650 + $5,500** ([HHS/ASPE](https://aspe.hhs.gov/topics/poverty-economic-mobility/poverty-guidelines), Jan 2025). At 3% inflation the projected 2024 base overshoots by ~2%, overstating subsidies and placing the 400% cliff ~$1,300 too high for a 1-person household. Rebase to 2025.

**U2. Roth conversions do not stop when Social Security starts early.** `RULES_AND_METHODOLOGY.md` §8 says "Conversions stop once Social Security starts (by design)". Code ([App.jsx:2608–2619](src/App.jsx:2608); couple [:2997–2999](src/App.jsx:2997)) only conditions the 65+ window on the claim age — a claimant at 62–64 with `conversionMid > 0` keeps converting while receiving benefits (probe: conversions of ~$34K at ages 62–64 alongside SS). The tax math on the overlap is computed correctly; decide the intended behavior and align code or spec.

**U3. SS claim age is not clamped at 70 for the start year.** Docs say claim age "is clamped to the legal window 62–70"; the engine clamps only the *benefit factor* at 70 — typing 75 delays benefits to 75 at the age-70 factor (probe: $0 benefits through age 74). The engine's behavior is actually a defensible model of a late claim; the docs are wrong, and the UI has no max on the field ([App.jsx:2414](src/App.jsx:2414), [:2577–2580](src/App.jsx:2577); `NumberInput` has no min/max). Fix docs + add a UI hint/max.

**U4. Time-zero debt payoff realizes gains that are never taxed.** [App.jsx:2434–2440](src/App.jsx:2434) (couple [:3332–3338](src/App.jsx:3332)) sells taxable to pay credit-card debt, reduces basis by the untaxed-gain amount, but assesses no tax in any year — the gain vanishes. Small at realistic debt levels; either tax it in year 1 or fold the payoff into the year-1 solve.

**U5. Couple-mode conversion cap over-reserves the RMD.** `personConversionTarget` reserves the **full** RMD from the 401(k) ([App.jsx:3001–3002](src/App.jsx:3001)); the individual engine correctly nets out what the IRA can cover ([:2634–2635](src/App.jsx:2634)). Conservative (never converts an RMD) but inconsistently caps couple conversions when the IRA is large.

## 4. 📋 Assumptions Not Documented (beyond the ⚠️ flags already in RULES §15)

1. **HSA pays pre-65 ACA premiums tax-free.** The healthcare portion of spending (mostly marketplace premiums pre-65) is offset dollar-for-dollar from the HSA ([App.jsx:2691–2692](src/App.jsx:2691)). Health-insurance premiums are generally **not** qualified §223(d) expenses before 65 (exceptions: COBRA, unemployment, LTC). Post-65 use for Medicare premiums + IRMAA is legitimate. Overstates HSA usefulness for early retirees; document or restrict.
2. **First-RMD Required Beginning Date.** The April-1-of-following-year deferral option for the first RMD is not modeled (RMD taken in the age-73/75 year). Conservative; say so.
3. **Contribution timing / part-year contributions.** Contributions are end-of-year (earn no return in their contribution year — ordinary-annuity convention), and the retirement-age year has **zero** contributions (no part-year accrual for a mid-year retirement).
4. **ACA below 100% FPL.** The model grants the 2.1% band to any MAGI below 133% FPL, including <100% where the PTC doesn't exist (Medicaid/NY Essential Plan territory). Harmless proxy, but undocumented.
5. **Monte Carlo median vs deterministic path.** MC draws Normal(post-return μ, σ): volatility drag (~σ²/2 ≈ 0.4%/yr at default 9%) means the MC **median** sits below the deterministic projection by construction. Users comparing the two should know the deterministic line is an above-median outcome.
6. **Unbounded MC draws.** Returns are not floored; at extreme user volatility (σ ≳ 30%) a draw below −100% can produce a negative account balance. Clamp draws (e.g., at −95%) for robustness.
7. **Individual-mode MFJ "household mirror".** With filing status MFJ in individual mode, both household members turn 65 when the primary does (2× senior deductions, 2× IRMAA enrollees). Noted in `CALCULATION_MODEL.md`, absent from RULES/UI.
8. **NIIT investment income.** Cash interest is now modeled as income but is excluded from the NIIT base (NII ≈ LTCG only); understates NIIT for large cash balances above the MAGI threshold.
9. **Rule of 55 calendar nuance.** Eligibility is `retirementAge ≥ 55` on integer ages; separating in the calendar year you *turn* 55 (while still 54) qualifies in real law but not in the model.
10. **ACA $2,000 out-of-pocket floor** ([App.jsx:448–450](src/App.jsx:448)) is a fixed nominal amount that never inflates.
